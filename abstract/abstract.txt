Parkinson disease disrupts motor control and often changes how a person walks, so gait may offer an objective way to measure motor severity. Yet a gait score can correlate with symptoms and still fail when used repeatedly over time. This study tested whether a fixed eight measure gait deviation score, built from an age and height adjusted healthy control reference without using Parkinson outcomes, captured gait specific severity beyond walking speed and survived separate validation tests. We analyzed 62 people with Parkinson disease and 124 walking observations from WearGait PD. Participant clustered models tested the association with gait specific MDS UPDRS severity. We also resampled the healthy reference 2,000 times, compared the score with a speed only model across 100 repeated participant grouped five fold validations, checked reconstruction of all eight gait measures on 252 matched trials, and applied the unchanged score to visits at least six months apart. The score was associated with greater severity with a standardized effect of 0.301 and a 95% confidence interval of 0.089 to 0.513, with q = 0.0055. All 2,000 reference resamples kept the same association direction. The extended model improved participant severity ranking in 98% of repeated validations, with a median Spearman improvement of 0.0474, but exact score prediction did not improve consistently. All eight reconstructed measures passed the planned agreement criteria. Long interval stability did not meet the planned standard. SelfPace ICC was 0.647 and HurriedPace ICC was 0.282. These results show that the score contains consistent severity ranking information beyond gait speed and can be reconstructed accurately, but association and analytical agreement alone do not prove that a digital gait measure is stable enough for longitudinal biomarker use.
