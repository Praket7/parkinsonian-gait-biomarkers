Parkinson disease alters basal ganglia circuits that help scale and automate movement, and these changes can appear in gait. Yet a gait measure can correlate with clinical severity without being sufficiently validated for longitudinal biomarker use. Rather than treating association as validation, this study tested one fixed outcome-blind gait deviation score referenced to healthy controls across distinct tests of association, robustness, measurement agreement, and longitudinal stability.

The score quantified multivariate deviation across eight gait features from an age- and height-adjusted healthy-control reference without using Parkinson severity outcomes. The primary analysis included 62 participants with Parkinson disease. The score was associated with gait-specific MDS-UPDRS severity, with standardized effect 0.301, 95% CI 0.089 to 0.513, and q = 0.0055. After walking-speed adjustment, the standardized effect was 0.279, 95% CI 0.083 to 0.475, with q = 0.0053. The association retained the same direction in all 2,000 healthy-control reference resamples. Participant-level Spearman correlation improved in 98 of 100 grouped validations, with median ΔSpearman = +0.047. All eight reconstructed inputs passed prespecified analytical-agreement criteria across 252 matched raw-walkway trials.

Despite this cross-sectional and analytical evidence, the same fixed score failed the prespecified stability criterion across visits separated by at least six months, which required the lower 95% confidence bound for ICC(A,1) to reach 0.80. Self-paced ICC(A,1) was 0.647 and hurried-pace ICC(A,1) was 0.282. Because visits were at least six months apart, this reflects long-interval stability rather than controlled short-term repeatability and may include true clinical or medication-related change.

These findings show that clinical association, analytical agreement, and better severity ranking do not by themselves establish longitudinal biomarker stability. A digital gait measure can satisfy several validation layers and still fail an important longitudinal criterion.
