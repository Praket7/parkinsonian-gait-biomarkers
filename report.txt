Testing a Gait Measure for Parkinsonian Severity and Longitudinal Use

Parkinson disease disrupts motor control, and those changes often appear in how a person walks. This study asked whether an objective gait score could measure Parkinsonian motor severity beyond walking speed and whether that same score was dependable enough for repeated use over time.

A fixed score was built from eight gait measures by comparing each person with an age and height adjusted healthy reference. Parkinson outcomes were not used to build that reference. The primary analysis included 62 people with Parkinson disease. The score was associated with greater gait specific severity, with a standardized effect of 0.301, a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.089 to 0.513, and a q value of 0.0055. The association remained after adjusting for walking speed. All 2,000 healthy reference resamples kept the same direction, and the score improved severity ranking beyond speed in 98 percent of participant grouped validation runs. All eight gait measures were also reconstructed successfully from raw walkway data across 252 matched trials.

The hardest test produced a different result. When the unchanged score was applied to visits at least six months apart, it failed the planned stability standard. The stability ICC was 0.647 for self paced walking and 0.282 for hurried walking. Because these visits were months apart, this does not measure short term repeatability and may include real changes in disease or medication state.

Recent Parkinson research shows that digital gait measures need separate evidence for reliability, clinical meaning, longitudinal behavior, and external validation. This project matters because it demonstrates that a strong association is not enough. The score captures consistent severity ranking information beyond walking speed, but more testing is needed before it can be trusted as a longitudinal biomarker.
